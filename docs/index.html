Boulder Lens · concept prototype

Video feedback on climbing technique

Real reports generated from phone videos filmed by hand at a climbing gym. The engine tracks the climber's body, compensates for the moving camera, and turns what it sees into feedback: strengths first, one thing to work on, clickable moments, measurements – and an honest list of what it could not judge.

Top rope – a beginner

Rope climbing is where feedback can be most concrete: the route is fixed, so mistakes are easier to name than on a boulder, where the "right" beta depends on the climber's body. This video is also the hardest case for the engine: the climber is small in the frame and the camera pans a lot.

What a coach sees

Hands at chest height with bent arms, hips away from the wall, standing upright instead of pushing with the legs – and a fall at the end.

What the engine sees today

The climber is small in the frame, so the engine finds them first and analyses a zoomed crop from the full-resolution video. Rope mode then flags bent arms while holding a position (twice, 1.2–2.4 s and 3.6–7.6 s), shows a tip on the video, and leaves the fall at the end out of scoring. Hips away from the wall can't be measured from behind yet.

What went well

  • Not enough visible data to name strengths.

What to work on

Bent arms while holding

When holding a position, hang on straight arms. Legs and hips do the work, arms just hold.
